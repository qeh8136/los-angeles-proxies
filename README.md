## Common mistakes when buying Los Angeles proxies

### Treating “California” and “Los Angeles” as identical

California coverage is not the same thing as verified city-level placement. If location granularity affects the outcome, get confirmation before purchase and test the assigned IPs.

### Buying the lowest price without checking billing units

One provider may quote per IP, another per GB, another per port, and another as an effective monthly rate for a quarterly commitment. Compare the final cost for your real request volume and number of persistent sessions.

### Ignoring minimum order size

A 50-IP package may offer good per-IP pricing but poor value for someone who only needs two IPs. Match the plan size to the workload, not to the excitement generated by a bulk discount.

### Assuming a proxy alone reproduces a local user’s experience

IP location is only one signal. Browser language, cookies, device type, account status, consent settings, and platform personalization all influence what a site displays. Document your test conditions so your results are interpretable later.

### Failing to test location and reputation before scale

A static IP is useful precisely because it remains associated with your workflow. Check its location, connectivity, and suitability early. Changing a large established setup after launch is much less fun than spending twenty minutes validating it at the start.
